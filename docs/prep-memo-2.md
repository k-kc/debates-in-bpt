# Prep Memo #2: Baldwin and the Tradition

## Assignment Overview

In this two-part assessment, you will analyze James Baldwin's "Down at the Cross: Letter from a Region in My Mind" (1963) genealogically. You'll prepare a memo that traces Baldwin's engagement with Black political thought, then defend your analysis in a graded seminar discussion.

---

## Part 1: Prep Memo

**Due:** Wednesday, April 8 at 11:59 PM  
**Length:** 3–4 pages  
**Format:** Chicago

### Guiding Questions

Your memo should address all of the following:

**On Historical Urgency**
- What specific historical problem is Baldwin confronting in this essay? Move beyond general categories to identify the particular crisis, condition, or challenge that makes his intervention urgent and distinctive.

**On Intellectual Inheritance**
- Which figures from Units 1–2 is Baldwin drawing on to think about this problem? Identify explicit references, thematic echoes, and conceptual foundations from Douglass, Du Bois, Wells, or other thinkers from the tradition.

**On Inherited Resources**
- What aspects of the inherited tradition remain adequate to address Baldwin's specific moment? Where does earlier Black political thought provide sufficient intellectual resources for his analysis?

**On Innovation**
- What does Baldwin have to invent or argue for that the tradition had not yet needed to address? Identify the new concepts, arguments, or insights he develops in response to problems specific to his moment (1963).

### Tips for Success

- Ground your analysis in specific passages from Baldwin. Use direct quotation to support claims about what he is arguing and what he is inheriting.
- Distinguish between what Baldwin explicitly states and what he assumes or implies.
- Think genealogically: Baldwin is not simply applying the tradition; he is transforming it in response to new historical conditions.
- Attend to nuance. Avoid reducing Baldwin's position to a simple "yes" or "no" about earlier thinkers.

## Part 2: Graded Seminar Discussion

**Date:** Thursday, April 9 (Full Period)

In seminar, you will defend your analysis, engage with peers' interpretations, and develop your genealogical thinking in real time.

### Evaluation Criteria

Your seminar performance will be evaluated according to the following four dimensions:

**Evidence of Deep Reading (25%)**
- Ability to cite specific passages from Baldwin with accuracy
- Use of evidence to support claims about genealogy and innovation
- Distinction between Baldwin's explicit arguments and his underlying assumptions

**Charitable Engagement (25%)**
- Understanding of Baldwin's position within its historical context
- Explanation of the logic and urgency of his intervention
- Resistance to reductive or anachronistic interpretation

**Comparative Thinking (25%)**
- Engagement with how Baldwin relates to earlier thinkers you have studied
- Recognition of how Baldwin both inherits from and departs from the tradition
- Identification of patterns and tensions across Units 1, 2, and 3

**Intellectual Honesty (25%)**
- Acknowledgment of complexities and tensions in Baldwin's argument
- Willingness to revise thinking based on peer contributions
- Distinction between genuine inquiry and performance of knowledge

## What to Prepare

- **For the memo:** Close, annotated reading of Baldwin's essay. Have specific passages marked that you might cite.
- **For the seminar:** Be ready to discuss your genealogical analysis, listen carefully to peers, and ask clarifying questions.
